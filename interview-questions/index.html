    Project Manager Interview Questions and Answers¶

    179 questions with the correct answer and a short explanation, organised from beginner (Level 1) to advanced (Level 4). Every question comes from a lesson in the free Project Manager Mastery Path, so you can open the lesson to go deeper.

    Practise, don't just read

    Each lesson also ends with an interactive quiz, and the final quiz tests all four levels. Try to answer each question below before reading the answer.

    Level 1 · Entry¶

    01 · What Is Project Management?¶

    Q1. What defines a project?

    Answer: A temporary endeavor with a unique outcome, defined start and end

    Versus operations.

    Q2. What is the triple constraint?

    Answer: Scope, time and cost, with quality affected by trade-offs

    Changing one affects others.

    Q3. What does a project manager do?

    Answer: Plans, coordinates and controls work to deliver the agreed outcome

    Leadership and control.

    Q4. How differ project, program and portfolio?

    Answer: Project: one outcome; program: related projects; portfolio: investments aligned to strategy

    Hierarchy.

    02 · Project Lifecycle Basics¶

    Q5. What are the classic phases?

    Answer: Initiating, planning, executing, monitoring/controlling, closing

    Process groups.

    Q6. Which phase ends with lessons learned and handover?

    Answer: Closing

    Formal closure.

    Q7. What authorizes a project formally?

    Answer: The project charter

    Sponsor signs.

    Q8. What happens in monitoring and controlling?

    Answer: Tracking performance against the plan and managing changes

    Runs throughout.

    03 · Scope Management Basics¶

    Q9. What is scope creep?

    Answer: Uncontrolled additions to scope without adjusting time, cost or resources

    Change control prevents it.

    Q10. What is a WBS?

    Answer: A hierarchical decomposition of deliverables into manageable work packages

    100% rule.

    Q11. What does the 100% rule say?

    Answer: The WBS includes all the work in scope, and nothing outside it

    Completeness.

    Q12. What does a scope statement include?

    Answer: Deliverables, boundaries, assumptions and acceptance criteria

    Shared understanding.

    04 · Scheduling Basics¶

    Q13. Tasks A(3d) → B(4d) and A → C(2d), then B and C → D(5d). What is the critical path length?

    print(3+4+5)

    Answer: 12

    A-B-D is the longest path. Verified by running it.

    Q14. A task has early start 5, duration 4, late finish 14. What is its total float?

    print(14-(5+4))

    Answer: 5

    LF − EF. Verified by running it.

    Q15. What is the critical path?

    Answer: The longest sequence of dependent tasks, determining the minimum duration

    Zero float.

    Q16. What is a milestone?

    Answer: A zero-duration marker for a significant point

    Visibility.

    05 · Budget & Cost Basics¶

    Q17. A project budget at completion is 100000 and 40% is complete. Planned value at that point is 40000. Actual cost is 45000. What is the cost variance (EV−AC)?

    print(100000*0.4-45000)

    Answer: -5000.0

    Negative = over budget. Verified by running it.

    Q18. What is contingency reserve?

    Answer: Budget for identified risks, managed by the project manager

    Known unknowns.

    Q19. What is management reserve?

    Answer: Budget for unknown unknowns, released by management

    Unknown unknowns.

    Q20. What is a baseline?

    Answer: The approved version of scope, schedule and cost used for comparison

    Change control.

    06 · Risk Management Basics¶

    Q21. What is a risk?

    Answer: An uncertain event that could affect objectives positively or negatively

    Future uncertainty.

    Q22. What is an issue?

    Answer: A risk that has occurred and needs resolution

    Present problem.

    Q23. A risk has probability 30% and impact 50000. What is the expected monetary value?

    print(0.3*50000)

    Answer: 15000.0

    EMV = P × I. Verified by running it.

    Q24. Which responses apply to threats?

    Answer: Avoid, mitigate, transfer, accept

    Opportunities: exploit, enhance, share.

    07 · Stakeholder Management Basics¶

    Q25. What does a stakeholder register capture?

    Answer: Who stakeholders are, their interests, influence and engagement strategy

    Identify early.

    Q26. A high-power, high-interest stakeholder should be?

    Answer: Managed closely

    Power/interest grid.

    Q27. What reduces stakeholder surprises?

    Answer: Regular tailored communication and expectation management

    Proactive.

    Q28. What is the main stakeholder engagement goal?

    Answer: Move stakeholders toward supportive engagement

    Engagement assessment.

    08 · Communication Plans¶

    Q29. A team of 8 people has how many communication channels (n(n−1)/2)?

    print(8*7//2)

    Answer: 28

    Channels grow quickly. Verified by running it.

    Q30. What does a communication plan say?

    Answer: Who needs what information, when, how and from whom

    Audience-based.

    Q31. When is face-to-face best?

    Answer: For complex, sensitive or ambiguous topics

    Rich channel.

    Q32. What is escalation?

    Answer: Raising issues beyond your authority to the right decision maker

    Defined path.

    09 · Tools Overview (Jira, MS Project, Asana)¶

    Q33. What do PM tools like Gantt charts show?

    Answer: Task timelines and dependencies

    Visual schedule.

    Q34. What does a Kanban board show?

    Answer: Work items flowing through stages with WIP limits

    Flow.

    Q35. What matters most when choosing a tool?

    Answer: Fit to the team's process and adoption, not features

    Usability.

    Q36. What is a single source of truth?

    Answer: One authoritative place for plans and status

    Avoid divergence.

    10 · Project — Full Project Charter & Schedule¶

    Q37. What does the charter and schedule project combine?

    Answer: Objectives, scope, stakeholders, milestones and a schedule

    Level 1 project.

    Q38. What should success criteria be?

    Answer: Measurable and agreed with the sponsor

    Clarity.

    Q39. What does the charter name?

    Answer: The project manager's authority and sponsor

    Mandate.

    Q40. How validate the schedule?

    Answer: Review with the team and check dependencies and resources

    Bottom-up.

    Level 2 · Intermediate¶

    01 · Agile Project Management¶

    Q41. What does a sprint produce?

    Answer: A potentially releasable increment of working product

    Time-boxed.

    Q42. What does a PM do in agile?

    Answer: Facilitates, removes impediments and aligns on outcomes

    Servant leadership.

    Q43. What is velocity?

    Answer: Work completed per sprint, used for forecasting

    Trend not target.

    Q44. What is a retrospective?

    Answer: A regular team reflection to improve the process

    Continuous improvement.

    02 · Advanced Scheduling¶

    Q45. PERT estimates: optimistic 4, most likely 6, pessimistic 14. Expected duration?

    print((4+4*6+14)/6)

    Answer: 7.0

    (O+4M+P)/6. Verified by running it.

    Q46. For the same PERT estimate, what is the standard deviation (P−O)/6?

    print(round((14-4)/6,2))

    Answer: 1.67

    Spread of uncertainty. Verified by running it.

    Q47. What is resource leveling?

    Answer: Adjusting the schedule to resolve resource overallocation

    May extend duration.

    Q48. What is fast tracking?

    Answer: Doing tasks in parallel that were planned in sequence (adds risk)

    Compression.

    03 · Earned Value Management Basics¶

    Q49. PV 50000, EV 45000, AC 48000. SPI and CPI (to 2 decimals)?

    print(round(45000/50000,2), round(45000/48000,2))

    Answer: 0.9 0.94

    SPI = EV/PV; CPI = EV/AC. Verified by running it.

    Q50. BAC 200000, CPI 0.8. Estimate at completion (BAC/CPI)?

    print(200000/0.8)

    Answer: 250000.0

    Current efficiency continues. Verified by running it.

    Q51. BAC 200000, EV 80000, AC 100000. Estimate to complete when the variance is atypical (EAC = AC + BAC − EV)?

    print(100000+200000-80000)

    Answer: 220000

    One-off overrun. Verified by running it.

    Q52. What is TCPI to finish on budget: (BAC−EV)/(BAC−AC)?

    print((200000-80000)/(200000-100000))

    Answer: 1.2

    Required efficiency. Verified by running it.

    04 · Risk Management Deep Dive¶

    Q53. What is a risk register?

    Answer: A living list of risks with probability, impact, owner and responses

    Review regularly.

    Q54. What is a risk trigger?

    Answer: An early warning sign that a risk is about to occur

    Monitor.

    Q55. What is a residual risk?

    Answer: The risk remaining after a response

    Accept or respond.

    Q56. What is a secondary risk?

    Answer: A new risk created by a risk response

    Evaluate.

    05 · Procurement Basics¶

    Q57. What does a fixed-price contract place on the seller?

    Answer: Cost overrun risk

    Scope clarity needed.

    Q58. What does time-and-materials suit?

    Answer: Uncertain scope where flexibility matters

    Cap it.

    Q59. What is an RFP?

    Answer: A request for proposals, asking vendors how they'd solve a problem

    Solicitation.

    Q60. How evaluate vendors?

    Answer: Using weighted criteria agreed before reviewing proposals

    Objectivity.

    06 · Quality Management Basics¶

    Q61. What is quality assurance vs quality control?

    Answer: QA improves processes; QC inspects results

    Prevention vs detection.

    Q62. What is the cost of quality?

    Answer: Prevention and appraisal plus failure costs

    Prevent early.

    Q63. What does a Pareto chart show?

    Answer: A few causes account for most defects (80/20)

    Focus.

    Q64. What are acceptance criteria?

    Answer: Conditions a deliverable must meet to be accepted

    Defined upfront.

    07 · Change Management for Projects¶

    Q65. What does a change control process do?

    Answer: Evaluates and approves or rejects changes with impact analysis

    Control scope.

    Q66. Who approves significant changes?

    Answer: A change control board or sponsor per governance

    Authority.

    Q67. What should a change request include?

    Answer: Description, rationale, impacts on scope, schedule, cost and risk

    Informed decision.

    Q68. What is the risk of informal change?

    Answer: Scope creep and lost baseline integrity

    Document.

    08 · Team Management & Conflict Resolution¶

    Q69. What's a healthy way to handle conflict?

    Answer: Address it early, focus on interests and problem-solve together

    Collaborate.

    Q70. Which conflict style is best long term?

    Answer: Collaborating (problem solving)

    Win-win.

    Q71. What are Tuckman's stages?

    Answer: Forming, storming, norming, performing

    Team development.

    Q72. What builds team performance?

    Answer: Clear goals, trust and defined roles

    Foundations.

    09 · Reporting & Dashboards¶

    Q73. What makes a dashboard useful?

    Answer: Few, clear metrics tied to decisions, updated consistently

    Actionable.

    Q74. What does RAG status mean?

    Answer: Red/Amber/Green indicators of health, defined by thresholds

    Objective definitions.

    Q75. What is a watermelon report?

    Answer: Green outside but red inside: status hides real problems

    Honest reporting.

    Q76. What do sponsors want from reports?

    Answer: Progress, risks and decisions needed

    Concise.

    10 · Project — Agile Plan with Sprint Schedule¶

    Q77. What does the agile sprint plan project combine?

    Answer: Backlog, sprint goal, capacity, dependencies and risks

    Level 2 project.

    Q78. How compute sprint capacity?

    Answer: Available person-days minus time off and meetings, adjusted by focus factor

    Realistic.

    Q79. What should a sprint goal be?

    Answer: A coherent outcome the team commits to

    Focus.

    Q80. What do you review at sprint end?

    Answer: The increment (review) and the way of working (retrospective)

    Inspect/adapt.

    Level 3 · Advanced¶

    01 · Program Management¶

    Q81. What is program management?

    Answer: Coordinating related projects to realize benefits not achievable separately

    Benefits focus.

    Q82. What does a program manager focus on?

    Answer: Benefits realization, interdependencies and governance

    Beyond delivery.

    Q83. What is a program benefits register?

    Answer: Expected benefits with owners, measures and timing

    Track value.

    Q84. What is a dependency across projects?

    Answer: One project's output or timing affecting another

    Manage actively.

    02 · Portfolio Management Basics¶

    Q85. What is portfolio management?

    Answer: Selecting and prioritizing investments to align with strategy

    Right projects.

    Q86. How prioritize a portfolio?

    Answer: Weighted criteria on strategic fit, value, risk and capacity

    Transparent.

    Q87. What is capacity-constrained prioritization?

    Answer: Selecting only what resources can deliver

    Limit WIP.

    Q88. When stop a project?

    Answer: When it no longer supports strategy or its value case collapses

    Sunk cost fallacy.

    03 · Advanced Risk & Issue Management¶

    Q89. What is a risk burndown?

    Answer: A trend of remaining risk exposure over time

    Visible risk.

    Q90. How manage an issue?

    Answer: Assign an owner, due date and escalate if blocked

    Resolve.

    Q91. What is a risk appetite?

    Answer: The amount of risk an organization is willing to accept

    Informs responses.

    Q92. What is a Monte Carlo simulation used for?

    Answer: Estimating schedule or cost outcomes with uncertainty ranges

    Probabilistic.

    04 · Vendor & Contract Management¶

    Q93. What do contracts need to protect?

    Answer: Scope, price, deliverables, acceptance, change and exit terms

    Clarity.

    Q94. What is an SLA?

    Answer: A service level agreement setting measurable service targets

    Performance.

    Q95. How manage vendor performance?

    Answer: Regular reviews against agreed metrics and clear escalation

    Governance.

    Q96. What is a statement of work?

    Answer: A document describing the work, deliverables and timeline

    Scope.

    05 · PMO Fundamentals¶

    Q97. What does a PMO provide?

    Answer: Standards, governance, tools and support for projects

    Enable.

    Q98. What types of PMO exist?

    Answer: Supportive, controlling and directive

    Degree of control.

    Q99. What's a PMO failure mode?

    Answer: Bureaucracy without value

    Add value.

    Q100. How show PMO value?

    Answer: Improved delivery predictability and benefits realized

    Outcomes.

    06 · Scaling Agile (SAFe, LeSS)¶

    Q101. What does SAFe aim to do?

    Answer: Coordinate many agile teams through shared cadence and planning

    Scaling.

    Q102. What is a program increment (PI)?

    Answer: A fixed time-box for planning and delivery across teams

    Cadence.

    Q103. What is a common scaling risk?

    Answer: Process overhead overwhelming team autonomy

    Keep light.

    Q104. What enables scaling?

    Answer: Clear architecture, alignment and decentralized decisions

    Coordination.

    07 · Advanced Stakeholder Management¶

    Q105. How manage a resistant senior stakeholder?

    Answer: Understand their concerns, involve them and align on outcomes

    Engagement.

    Q106. What is a stakeholder influence map?

    Answer: A view of relationships and influence among stakeholders

    Navigate.

    Q107. What builds sponsor support?

    Answer: Honest updates, decision-ready options and delivering results

    Trust.

    Q108. What do you do about a hidden opponent?

    Answer: Surface concerns early through conversation

    Address.

    08 · Project Recovery¶

    Q109. What's the first step in project recovery?

    Answer: Assess the real status and root causes honestly

    Diagnose.

    Q110. What options exist in recovery?

    Answer: Re-baseline, reduce scope, add resources wisely or stop

    Choose with sponsor.

    Q111. Why not just add people to a late project?

    Answer: It adds communication overhead and ramp-up (Brooks's law)

    Be careful.

    Q112. What sustains recovery?

    Answer: Clear plan, frequent checkpoints and visible wins

    Rebuild trust.

    09 · Advanced Metrics (EVM Deep Dive & Forecasting)¶

    Q113. What does a burndown chart show?

    Answer: Remaining work over time

    Trend.

    Q114. What does cycle time measure?

    Answer: Time from starting work to finishing it

    Flow metric.

    Q115. What does throughput measure?

    Answer: Items completed per period

    Forecast.

    Q116. What's wrong with measuring individual output?

    Answer: Encourages gaming and harms collaboration

    Team-level metrics.

    10 · Project — Program Plan for 3 Related Projects¶

    Q117. What does the program plan project combine?

    Answer: Governance, dependencies, benefits, risks and integrated schedule

    Level 3 project.

    Q118. How integrate schedules?

    Answer: Link cross-project milestones and manage the critical path

    Dependencies.

    Q119. What belongs in program governance?

    Answer: Decision rights, steering committee and escalation

    Oversight.

    Q120. How track program benefits?

    Answer: Baseline, measure and report them after delivery

    Realization.

    Level 4 · Master¶

    01 · Leading PMOs¶

    Q121. What does leading a PMO involve?

    Answer: Building capability, governance and value, not just templates

    Strategic.

    Q122. How measure PMO maturity?

    Answer: Against a maturity model with capability and outcome indicators

    Improve.

    Q123. How gain adoption of standards?

    Answer: Co-create with delivery teams and show benefits

    Pull not push.

    Q124. What shows PMO impact?

    Answer: On-time, on-budget delivery and benefit realization

    Business outcomes.

    02 · Enterprise Portfolio Management¶

    Q125. What is the aim of enterprise portfolio management?

    Answer: Maximizing strategic value within constraints across all investments

    Value.

    Q126. What is a funding model shift?

    Answer: From project funding to persistent product/value-stream funding

    Agility.

    Q127. How handle changing priorities?

    Answer: Regular portfolio reviews with transparent trade-offs

    Adapt.

    Q128. What is a key portfolio metric?

    Answer: Value delivered versus plan and capacity utilization

    Insight.

    03 · Digital Transformation Programs¶

    Q129. Why do digital transformations fail?

    Answer: People and change issues more than technology

    Adoption.

    Q130. What is critical for transformation programs?

    Answer: Clear vision, sponsorship, change management and incremental value

    Deliver value early.

    Q131. How reduce risk?

    Answer: Pilot, learn and scale in stages

    Iterate.

    Q132. What measures success?

    Answer: Business outcomes and adoption, not go-live

    Benefits.

    04 · Crisis & Turnaround Project Leadership¶

    Q133. First step in a crisis turnaround?

    Answer: Stabilize and gain clarity on the situation and priorities

    Triage.

    Q134. What does a leader do in a crisis?

    Answer: Communicate clearly, decide quickly and support the team

    Calm.

    Q135. What helps rebuild confidence?

    Answer: Short-term wins and transparent progress

    Momentum.

    Q136. What do you document after?

    Answer: Lessons learned and prevention actions

    Learn.

    05 · Executive Reporting & Governance¶

    Q137. What do executives want in reports?

    Answer: Concise status, risks, decisions and value

    Decision-oriented.

    Q138. What is governance?

    Answer: The framework for decision-making, accountability and oversight

    Clarity.

    Q139. What is a stage gate?

    Answer: A decision point to continue, change or stop

    Control investment.

    Q140. How avoid reporting theater?

    Answer: Use objective metrics and honest assessments

    Trust.

    06 · Building PM Culture & Capability¶

    Q141. How build PM capability?

    Answer: Training, mentoring, communities of practice and real stretch work

    Experience.

    Q142. What is a community of practice?

    Answer: A group sharing knowledge and improving a discipline

    Learning network.

    Q143. How grow culture?

    Answer: Role-model transparency and learning from failure

    Behavior.

    Q144. What do you measure?

    Answer: Delivery outcomes and capability growth

    Evidence.

    07 · AI Tools for Project Management¶

    Q145. How use AI tools responsibly in PM work?

    Answer: As assistants for drafts and analysis, verifying outputs and protecting confidential data

    Human judgement.

    Q146. A good AI use?

    Answer: Summarizing meeting notes and drafting risk lists for human review

    Augment.

    Q147. What is an AI risk in PM?

    Answer: Confident but wrong outputs and data leakage

    Verify.

    Q148. How adopt AI in a team?

    Answer: Pilot with guidelines, training and measured outcomes

    Learn.

    08 · Global/Distributed Team Project Management¶

    Q149. What helps global distributed teams?

    Answer: Clear communication norms, overlap hours and written decisions

    Async.

    Q150. How handle time zones fairly?

    Answer: Rotate inconvenient meeting times

    Inclusion.

    Q151. What is a cultural risk?

    Answer: Different communication styles misread as disagreement or disengagement

    Awareness.

    Q152. What builds trust remotely?

    Answer: Reliability, responsiveness and personal connection

    Relationships.

    09 · Career Growth: PM to Program/Portfolio Director¶

    Q153. What advances PM to director?

    Answer: Leading through others, strategic influence and business results

    Scope.

    Q154. What does a director focus on?

    Answer: Portfolio outcomes, talent and strategy

    Leverage.

    Q155. How build executive presence?

    Answer: Clarity, composure and a track record of results

    Credibility.

    Q156. What supports growth?

    Answer: Mentors, sponsors and stretch assignments

    Development.

    10 · Capstone — Full Program Charter & Governance Model¶

    Q157. What does the capstone combine?

    Answer: Program charter, governance, benefits and risk framework

    Level 4 project.

    Q158. What must a program charter state?

    Answer: Vision, benefits, scope, governance, funding and success measures

    Authorize.

    Q159. How set governance?

    Answer: Define roles, decision rights, cadences and escalation

    Clarity.

    Q160. How sustain a program?

    Answer: Track benefits and adapt with disciplined reviews

    Value.

    Mixed questions across all levels¶

    Q161. Tasks: A(4d) → B(6d), A → C(3d), B and C → D(2d). What is the project duration and which task has float?

    d={'A':4,'B':6,'C':3,'D':2}
print(4+6+2, 'C float', (4+6)-(4+3))

# Critical Path Method: forward pass, backward pass, float and the critical path.
TASKS = {            # name: (duration in days, predecessors)
    "Requirements": (5, []),
    "Design":       (7, ["Requirements"]),
    "Procure":      (10, ["Requirements"]),
    "Build":        (12, ["Design", "Procure"]),
    "Docs":         (6, ["Design"]),
    "Test":         (6, ["Build"]),
    "Train":        (4, ["Docs", "Build"]),
    "Go-live":      (1, ["Test", "Train"]),
}

def schedule(tasks):
    order, done = [], set()
    while len(order) < len(tasks):                      # topological order
        for t, (_, preds) in tasks.items():
            if t not in done and all(p in done for p in preds):
                order.append(t); done.add(t)
    es, ef = {}, {}
    for t in order:                                     # forward pass
        dur, preds = tasks[t]
        es[t] = max((ef[p] for p in preds), default=0)
        ef[t] = es[t] + dur
    end = max(ef.values())
    succ = {t: [u for u, (_, ps) in tasks.items() if t in ps] for t in tasks}
    ls, lf = {}, {}
    for t in reversed(order):                           # backward pass
        lf[t] = min((ls[s] for s in succ[t]), default=end)
        ls[t] = lf[t] - tasks[t][0]
    float_ = {t: ls[t] - es[t] for t in tasks}
    return order, es, ef, ls, lf, float_, end

def critical_path(order, float_, tasks):
    return [t for t in order if float_[t] == 0]

order, es, ef, ls, lf, fl, end = schedule(TASKS)
print(f"{'task':13s}{'ES':>4s}{'EF':>4s}{'LS':>4s}{'LF':>4s}{'float':>6s}")
for t in order:
    print(f"{t:13s}{es[t]:4d}{ef[t]:4d}{ls[t]:4d}{lf[t]:4d}{fl[t]:6d}")
print("duration:", end, "days")
print("critical path:", " -> ".join(critical_path(order, fl, TASKS)))
print("slack tasks:", ", ".join(f"{t}({fl[t]})" for t in order if fl[t] > 0))

# What if Procure slips by 3 days?
slipped = dict(TASKS); slipped["Procure"] = (13, ["Requirements"])
_, _, _, _, _, fl2, end2 = schedule(slipped)
print("with Procure +3d -> duration:", end2, "| critical:", " -> ".join(t for t in schedule(slipped)[0] if fl2[t] == 0))
```

Forward pass gives earliest times, the backward pass gives latest allowable times, and zero-float tasks form the critical path. A slip on a non-critical task consumes its float, but beyond it the path shifts. Verified by running the Python implementation.

# Earned value management, forecasts and a PERT estimate.
BAC = 500_000
REPORTS = [            # month, planned % complete, actual % complete, actual cost to date
    (1, 10, 10, 48_000), (2, 25, 22, 120_000), (3, 40, 33, 205_000), (4, 55, 44, 290_000),
]
def evm(month_row):
    m, planned, actual, ac = month_row
    pv, ev = BAC * planned / 100, BAC * actual / 100
    cpi, spi = ev / ac, ev / pv
    eac = BAC / cpi                              # current efficiency continues
    etc = eac - ac
    tcpi = (BAC - ev) / (BAC - ac)
    return {"PV": pv, "EV": ev, "AC": ac, "CV": ev - ac, "SV": ev - pv, "CPI": cpi, "SPI": spi, "EAC": eac, "ETC": etc, "VAC": BAC - eac, "TCPI": tcpi}

def status(cpi, spi):
    if cpi >= 0.95 and spi >= 0.95: return "GREEN"
    if cpi >= 0.85 and spi >= 0.85: return "AMBER"
    return "RED"

print("month   PV      EV      AC      CPI   SPI   EAC       status")
for r in REPORTS:
    e = evm(r)
    print(f"{r[0]:>5d} {e['PV']:7,.0f} {e['EV']:7,.0f} {e['AC']:7,.0f}  {e['CPI']:.2f}  {e['SPI']:.2f}  {e['EAC']:8,.0f}  {status(e['CPI'], e['SPI'])}")
last = evm(REPORTS[-1])
print(f"latest: CV={last['CV']:,.0f} SV={last['SV']:,.0f} VAC={last['VAC']:,.0f} TCPI={last['TCPI']:.2f}")
print("overrun forecast:", f"{-last['VAC'] / BAC * 100:.1f}% of BAC")

def pert(o, m, p):
    return (o + 4 * m + p) / 6, (p - o) / 6
phases = {"Design": (8, 10, 18), "Build": (20, 26, 44), "Test": (6, 8, 16)}
total_mean = sum(pert(*v)[0] for v in phases.values())
total_sd = sum(pert(*v)[1] ** 2 for v in phases.values()) ** 0.5
print(f"PERT total: {total_mean:.1f} days, sd {total_sd:.1f}; 95% range {total_mean - 2 * total_sd:.1f}-{total_mean + 2 * total_sd:.1f}")
```

EVM compares what was planned, earned and spent; CPI and SPI show efficiency, and EAC = BAC ÷ CPI assumes current efficiency continues. PERT adds expected values and combines variances for the range. The numbers are illustrative. Verified by running it.
